codeArbiter is easier to understand when you can inspect what it produces. This tour follows a
saved-search application from project-level planning to a focused CSV export. The documents are
readable outside a chat, and the example tests can be rerun without a model account.

**Evidence boundary:** the specification and plan below were created and validated by the native
artifact engine at the recorded source identity. They remain unapproved drafts. The Python RED
and GREEN results are actual local fixture runs. The project blueprint, conversation, host
comparison, review, and delivery story are explanatory examples, not captured host executions.
Nothing on this page approves work, changes your repository, or proves an installed release.

## From an idea to a project

Before a greenfield project has source, its architecture, delivery phases, and backlog need
separate explanations. Decompose creates three named Markdown documents and asks you to review
all three. A roadmap does not become an executable feature plan simply because both contain tasks.
The examples retain unresolved questions so you can see where your decision is still needed.

## From a requirement to an inspectable plan

For a qualified new full-lane workflow, the specification owns stable acceptance-criterion IDs.
The plan references them instead of creating a competing requirements list. Choose a criterion
below to see that connection. Open either native document to inspect its complete readable view.
The files need no JavaScript or network access when opened offline.

<ArtifactWorkbench />

A structural pass means the document's shape and references satisfy that validator. It does not
establish that a requirement is true, that a person approved it, or that implementation passed its
review. [Review specifications and plans](/guides/review-artifacts/) explains what you must inspect.

## Follow the delivery story

Use the stage controls to move at your own pace. With JavaScript unavailable, every stage remains
readable in document order. The fixture's two failing baseline assertions and three passing final
checks demonstrate one pure-function contract, not a complete application release.

## Keep project state when you change hosts

Command spelling and integrations differ by host. The shared repository records remain the place
to inspect your project. These panels compare documented entry points; they are not screenshots
of real sessions and do not qualify concurrent use or unsupported host versions.

## Understand a stop before retrying

A stopped task is not necessarily a broken installation. It may need an approval, a fresh read,
review evidence, or reconciliation of an interrupted operation. The explanations below preserve
those differences. An error is not permission to edit a hidden flag or manufacture a receipt.

<RecoveryExplorer />

Follow [Resume and recover](/guides/resume-and-recover/) with the actual diagnostic and operation
identity from your repository. For installation problems, begin with [Troubleshooting](/guides/troubleshooting/).

## Choose the next step

To evaluate the workflow, open the example documents and inspect their capture identities. To
use codeArbiter, [choose your host](/getting-started/choose-your-host/), install and trust the
adapter, and [prove a real block](/getting-started/quickstart/) in a disposable repository.
For guided, verifier-backed practice, use [Arbiter Academy](/academy/) in its published order.
The tour does not create an account, import progress, or store completion state.
