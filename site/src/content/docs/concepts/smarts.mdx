SMARTS is codeArbiter's six-lens design-quality rubric for material engineering solution choices.
It makes the reasons for a recommendation inspectable: the alternatives, six engineering lenses,
the evidence behind each judgment, and the factors the rubric does not cover. It is useful when a
change admits more than one plausible approach. It is not a numerical score that automatically
selects the winner, and it is not a command: it runs inside the workflows that already shape, plan,
fix and reconcile work.

SMARTS grants no approval. The surrounding lane determines who can make the decision. Interactive
reconciliation needs an explicit user choice. A requested sprint makes reversible method decisions
inside its existing scope, including close calls. Use SMARTS when a difficult trade-off warrants
comparison; routine steps need no scoring exercise. Neither caller gains authority beyond the
user's actual request, and a strong recommendation never stands in for a decision the lane requires.

## Three depths

Every evaluation uses the same lenses and verdicts; the workflow chooses how much of it to show.

- **Scan** — a lightweight six-lens fitness pass when a choice is material but does not need the
  full table. It names the material strengths, weaknesses, unknowns and decisive lenses.
- **Comparison** — the full option-by-option table. It appears when two or more materially
  plausible approaches exist, when you explicitly ask for a full SMARTS read, or when
  reconciliation or arbitration needs it. Asking for the full read changes the presentation only;
  it adds no command and no decision authority.
- **Delegated decision** — a requested sprint choosing a reversible in-scope method on your
  behalf and logging the reason in its sprint log.

Feature shaping compares approaches during brainstorming only when two or more are materially
plausible, before one is recommended. A single credible approach gets one line that says why no
alternative was credible, and no table; nobody invents a straw alternative to fill one. The
comparison dispatches no grader or scout and does no bulk read of plans or decisions. A
recommendation informs the lane's existing decision owner and does not authorize anything by
itself. The small lane scans only when a change has a material solution choice; otherwise it
states `SMARTS: no material solution choice` and adds no table. Debugging uses no SMARTS at all: a
fix compares remediation strategies only after the root cause is confirmed.

## Context before verdicts

A verdict means something only at a stated context. Before judging a material choice, codeArbiter
establishes just the context that makes the lenses meaningful: the decision itself, its scope and
horizon, and scale, failure, trust-boundary or non-SMARTS constraints only when they could change a
verdict. The context comes from existing project records, the active spec and your steering. It is
scope-sized, fail-soft and creates no new project or configuration file.

## Begin with recorded intent

Before sprint or specification-shaping scoring, the recorded-intent check asks whether the project
has already answered the question. The source order is an explicit user decision in the current
session, an unsuperseded decision-log entry, an accepted ADR, then the three project planning
artifacts. Project context and recorded open/deferred questions constrain the comparison at their
recorded level. Relevant indexes and headings come first; the check is not a bulk read of every
project document.

There are three useful results. **Answered** means follow the highest-ranked decision and cite it.
**Constrains** means carry that source into the affected comparison cells. **Silent** means no
record answers the question, so proceed and identify that absence. A missing planning or decision
directory alone is not a reason to stop this check.

A request to contradict recorded intent is different from silence. In an interactive lane it can
require reconciliation or supersession. A sprint surfaces a real contradiction rather than running
a mid-sprint reconcile session on its own. A recorded deferral whose re-evaluation trigger has
occurred is reopened and surfaced, not quietly treated as permission to choose anew.

This Step 0 applies to sprint and brainstorming. The arbitration skill, grader and
optional decision-challenger are explicitly exempt from that separate step: their work already
compares the conflicting record under the arbitration authority order. That exception does not
exempt them from the six-lens scoring rules.

## The Six Lenses

**Scalable** asks whether growth fits the design without an architectural rewrite. A facility for
millions of jobs is not automatically an advantage for a tightly bounded export. **Maintainable**
asks whether future developers can understand and change the complete solution, including its
operational dependencies. **Available** asks whether the capability is reachable when needed;
this is not a demand that every project implement high availability.

**Reliable** concerns correct, predictable and durable outcomes when work fails or repeats.
Availability and reliability can diverge: an endpoint can respond while returning an incorrect
result. **Testable** asks whether meaningful success and failure behavior can be checked with
repeatable tests. **Securable** asks whether the design can meet the project's security controls
without a retrofit. It is a design judgment, not a completed security review or bypass permission.

The same lenses apply to every option. The worked comparison below makes its assumptions visible
so a reader can disagree with a premise rather than merely disputing an adjective.

## Verdicts and Strength

Each cell begins with **Strong**, **Adequate**, **Weak**, **Indifferent** or **Unknown**. The
canonical format caps the cell at 25 words and its justification at 20. The justification names an
option property, project constraint or failure mode; “best practice” is not evidence. The rubric
rejects hedging adverbs: genuine uncertainty is an Unknown, not a softened adjective.

### Unknown is not Indifferent

The two verdicts answer different questions. **Indifferent** is a finding: the evidence shows the
lens does not distinguish the options at the stated context, so every option takes it. In a
single-option scan it means the lens is not material to this choice. **Unknown** is an admission:
the evidence needed to judge the lens is missing, contradictory or not yet established.

Every Unknown names its missing observation and states whether it is decision-critical, meaning that
observation could change which option is selected. An Unknown is never neutral and never quietly
becomes Indifferent, Adequate or a pass. A decision-critical Unknown blocks a strong recommendation,
blocks selecting the option that carries it, and is surfaced before any irreversible or
authority-bearing choice. A non-critical Unknown can stand only when the output names the missing
observation and why it does not control the decision. An Unknown cannot bypass a hard gate or
manufacture authority.

Recommendation strength is a separate field: **strong** when dominant lenses align cleanly,
non-SMARTS factors confirm and no option carries a decision-critical Unknown; **moderate** for a
qualified or single-lens advantage; and **tied** when no preferred option emerges. There is no weak
recommendation level. Counting Strong cells or converting labels into invented numbers would lose
the project's actual priorities. When lenses conflict, an explicit current or approved-spec priority
comes first, then a recorded project priority, then precedent labelled as precedent; with none of
those, a tie stays tied.

Cost, time-to-market, team-skill fit, vendor lock-in and political acceptability are
non-SMARTS considerations. They must remain visible alongside the table when relevant. A technically attractive
option can still be the wrong business choice. In the example, an established synchronous-response
decision must be respected before any new queue is considered.

## Where it runs

During reconciliation, prior decisions provide a `Precedent:` line with relevant IDs and the
observed lens pattern. Thin history cannot support an invented precedent, and precedent never
outranks an explicit higher-authority decision. A strong recommendation remains a recommendation
until the user makes or explicitly accepts the choice.

During a sprint, moderate and tied in-scope choices do not create new routine human
checkpoints. The sprint breaks a tie using its stated priorities and the existing conflict hierarchy.
It logs **low** confidence for moderate/tied choices and **high** for strong choices. An answered
recorded-intent choice is logged with high confidence and its citation. Confidence is not a
probability estimate or a verification result. Security boundaries, irreversible actions, overrides,
missing user authority, conflicting accepted decisions and merge remain actual stop boundaries.

## Maintain methods within the agreed outcome

Update implementation steps, verification runners and task decomposition through the existing
typed plan writer when they remain within the authorized outcome. Record the reason, preserve
stable references and user stops, and inspect concurrent work before changing an active task.
Ordinary method maintenance needs no separate artifact approval or method grant.

Reuse checks and review conclusions while their relevant source, requirements and inputs still
match. Recheck affected evidence after a material change, and keep new failures visible. A changed
receipt or session alone does not restart review. A different product outcome, spending outside
the agreed limit or an irreversible action still belongs to the actual decision owner.

## Audit a Verdict

Start with the evidence that could change the outcome. Are the options materially different, or is
one a deliberately weak alternative? Does each cell refer to the actual scale, deployment boundary
and failure behavior? Which lens is decisive, and is that priority stated in project records? Keep
unknown facts visible and ask for the missing observation rather than inventing a confident cell.

Next identify the caller and recorded outcome. Sprint reasoning belongs in
`.codearbiter/sprint-log.md`, with intent and confidence. Interactive arbitration decisions belong in
`.codearbiter/decisions/decision-log.md`, with user attribution. Its `Artifact-section-hash` binds
the cited source artifact section, not the log entry's own prose or the whole codebase. A changed
hash signals changed referenced text; it does not decide that the recorded choice is wrong.

A useful completion check is to explain the choice, its strongest counterargument, its source,
and the specific authority needed for the next action. Use [Run an autonomous sprint](/guides/autonomous-sprints/)
for execution, [ADRs and the decision log](/concepts/adrs/) for durable choices, and
[Auditability](/concepts/auditability/) for the later evidence packet. The exact comparison and
arbitration rules remain in the [grader](/reference/agents/grader/) and
[reconcile](/reference/commands/reconcile/) references.
